Six rules, and why each one is here

Most communities for designers turn into a feed, then into an argument, then into nothing. These are the rules we think stop that. If one of them is wrong, we'd rather hear it than keep it.

01

Answer the question that was actually asked

If someone asks how to price a project, answer that — not a lecture on why they should have asked something else.

Specific questions get specific answers. That's the whole reason this exists instead of a general chat.

02

A real number beats an opinion

"I charged $900 for a similar scope in 2025" is worth more here than "you should charge more."

If you don't have a number or a direct experience to add, a vote is often more useful than a reply.

03

No recruiting in the replies

If you're hiring, post a role. Don't DM someone who just answered a question to pitch them a job.

People stop answering questions honestly the moment answering becomes a sales funnel.

04

Critique the work, not the person

"This hierarchy is hard to scan" is feedback. "You clearly didn't think about hierarchy" is not — say the first one.

Portfolio reviews only work if people keep bringing real work to them.

05

Sessions have seats for a reason

Book a seat you intend to use. Cancel if you can't make it — someone on the waitlist gets it the moment you do.

A no-show costs a real seat that would've gone to someone else.

06

Pay data stays anonymous, always

Never post a number in a way that identifies the company or the person who reported it, even if you know both.

The pay ranges only stay honest if nobody has to worry about being traced back.

What happens when someone breaks one

Three steps, in order. A person decides each one, and we tell you which rule and which post.

1. Reviewed

A report goes to a person on the team, not a filter — usually within a day.

2. Decided

The content is kept, removed, or the account gets a note, depending on what actually happened.

3. Explained

If something of yours is removed, you're told which rule and which post — never a silent removal.

Removal is rare, and it's never silent. You keep your profile, your work, and your saved roles either way, because your career isn't ours to take.

Report something

Every post and reply has a report control. It goes to a person, not a filter.

Disagree with a rule?

Say so in Community. We've changed rules before because someone argued well.
